Practice Pack: 07 RAG and Vector Search#

Work this pack at least one day after reading the module. Write your answer before opening each solution; recognition is not retrieval.

1. Retrieval questions#

  1. When is RAG the wrong answer?
Answer When the information is stable and fits a deterministic query, when a model behavior change is needed, or when retrieval cannot provide the required evidence.
  1. What makes a chunk useful?
Answer It preserves enough meaning and metadata to answer a question without adding excessive irrelevant context.
  1. What does recall@k measure?
Answer The fraction of relevant items retrieved within the top k results.
  1. Why combine lexical and vector search?
Answer Lexical search catches exact terms and identifiers; vector search catches semantic similarity. Their errors differ.
  1. How do you separate retrieval from generation quality?
Answer Check whether the needed evidence appears in retrieved results before judging whether the model used it correctly.

2. Prediction exercises#

  1. If chunks are too large, what may happen to retrieval and generation?
Answer Retrieval becomes less precise and the prompt contains more irrelevant material, increasing cost and distraction.
  1. If recall@k improves but answer quality does not, what should you inspect?
Answer Reranking, prompt construction, evidence sufficiency, model grounding, and whether the evaluation labels the right failure.
  1. If a permission filter is applied after retrieval, what risk exists?
Answer Unauthorized content may have already influenced ranking or logging; permissions should constrain retrieval before content is exposed.

3. Debugging scenarios#

  1. The answer says 'not found' even though the right chunk exists at rank 20. What failure is that?
Answer A retrieval recall or cutoff failure; inspect chunking, query formulation, index recall, filtering, and k.
  1. The right chunks are retrieved but the answer cites the wrong one. What should you instrument?
Answer The exact prompt context, source identifiers, citation mapping, and model output against the retrieved evidence.
  1. A document update leaves old answers in the system. What operational capability is missing?
Answer Freshness and deletion handling across the source, index, cache, and evaluation data.

4. Short explanation prompts#

  1. Explain why chunking is an information-design problem.
Answer Chunk boundaries determine what can be retrieved together and whether the context contains a complete answer, not just how many tokens fit.
  1. Explain the retrieval-to-answer failure taxonomy.
Answer A system can fail during parsing, chunking, indexing, querying, ranking, prompt construction, generation, or citation; each needs different evidence.
  1. Explain why evaluation comes before tuning.
Answer Without a fixed evaluation set and failure labels, tuning only changes anecdotes and cannot show whether the system improved.

5. Implementation task#

Build a small retrieval harness over ten support documents. Compare fixed-size, sentence-aware, and hybrid retrieval; report recall@k and three labeled failure cases before changing the generator.

Do not open the rubric until you have a working attempt.

6. Rubric#

  • [ ] The evaluation questions and relevant chunks are explicit.
  • [ ] Chunking and retrieval changes are isolated.
  • [ ] Metrics are computed from hand-checked labels.
  • [ ] At least one failure is traced to retrieval and one to generation/prompting.
  • [ ] The final choice states a tradeoff.

7. Scoring#

  • 4 — Ready: answers are produced without notes, the implementation works, and the explanation names tradeoffs.
  • 3 — Practiced: one weak area remains; schedule a review in 7 days and redo that section.
  • 2 — Learning: revisit the module's first-pass path and retry the prediction/debugging sections tomorrow.
  • 1 — Not yet: ask for help or use the worked example, then restart with a smaller task.
